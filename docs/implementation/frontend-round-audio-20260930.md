# Q3 round audio ownership

Source review only. No executable, compiler, test, parser, generator or benchmark ran for this change.

The behavioral references are the TypeScript audio engine's `stopAll`, `resetRound` and `setListeners`, and application round restart in `src/app/bootstrap/application.ts`. A round stops active channels, source music and raw buses, clears listener and actor-position publication, and clears queued device PCM and resampling history. It keeps each old seat's mixer until the next listener publication. That publication reuses the matching seat's mixer and starts its paint time at the existing engine clock. Mixer RNG, channel allocation and internal state have their original owners throughout the transfer.

The C engine now owns inactive `{seat, mixer}` entries separately from active seat wrappers. Reset transfers the actual mixer pointer and releases the old reverb/environment wrapper. Listener publication transfers matching mixers into new wrappers and releases unused inactive mixers. The engine destructor and isolated restore cleanup own both inventories. Callback readiness reads the real engine and mixer callback, dispatch and destruction flags. The reset fences every actual owned mixer before dispatching stop notifications. Callback mutations cannot repopulate another stopped mixer. Callback destruction attempts cause reset failure while the engine and mixers remain allocated. Callback-producing loop, listener, environment and update entrypoints hold real engine operations; nested structural mutation cannot retire an executing owner.

`QAEN` version 2 records inactive mixers after the active seats and buses, using the ordinary full mixer codec. Decode checks the seat identity, unique destructor ownership, stopped voices, empty loops/loop mixes/positions/transmission/event publication, zero source offset and schedule order, raw end at paint time, and complete framing. It rejects version 1. Stable heap import rebinds the actual engine voice allocator and observer contexts in active and inactive mixers. Isolated failed restore scrubs nested callbacks before disposal. The global asset inventory visits both inventories and retains the original prepared asset references. Geometry publication updates the genuine inactive mixer callback binding as well as active seats.

Device round reset prepares a fresh output resampler, fences native queued playback with the existing SDL pause operation, clears queued and staged PCM, swaps the resampler, clears pump history, and restores the previous playback state. A real pump flag prevents reset during engine mixing callbacks. This does not provide a device checkpoint or a saved-output handoff contract.

Frontend actor audio IDs keep full-generation provenance after their active aliases retire. This permits the full mixer codec to describe an inactive mixer's old listener without treating the old actor as live. New live actors receive new monotonic IDs. The installed frontend aggregate still needs to encode these retired references against the saved actor generation history.

The installed frontend creates enabled mixers with no observer or diagnostic callbacks. The preexisting ordinary engine codec requires matching wrapper and mixer listeners; separately borrowed disabled mixers can retain a different listener and remain outside that codec admission. The frontend round transaction and its full source/network callers need their own source review. This packet does not establish complete rendering, native module, campaign or multiplayer parity.
